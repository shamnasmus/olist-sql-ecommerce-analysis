DATA QUALITY CHECKS
- Dates are stored correctly in YYYY-MM-DD format, so delivery-time 
  calculations will work directly.
- 2,965 orders have no delivery date (not yet delivered/cancelled).
- No duplicate order IDs found — data integrity is good.
- Order status breakdown: 96,478 delivered (97%), 1,107 shipped, 
  625 canceled, 609 unavailable, 314 invoiced, 301 processing, 
  5 created, 2 approved.
- Decision: filtering analysis to order_status = 'delivered' going 
  forward, since these represent completed transactions.

Q1: MONTHLY REVENUE
Revenue grew through 2017, peaking in Nov 2017 (~R$987K), likely 
Black Friday. 2018 stayed strong at R$800K-970K/month.

Q2: TOP PRODUCT CATEGORIES BY REVENUE
Top 3: health_beauty (R$1.23M), watches_gifts (R$1.17M), 
bed_bath_table (R$1.02M). Notably, watches_gifts has far fewer 
orders (5,495) than bed_bath_table (9,272) but similar revenue, 
suggesting a much higher average price per item.

Q3: REVENUE BY STATE AND CITY
Revenue is heavily concentrated in Brazil's Southeast region. São 
Paulo (SP) state alone generated R$5.07M (40,501 orders), about 40% 
of total revenue, more than 3x the next state (Rio de Janeiro, 
R$1.76M). At the city level, São Paulo city alone brought in 
R$1.86M (15,045 orders), nearly double Rio de Janeiro city. 
3 of the top 8 cities are within SP state itself (São Paulo, 
Campinas, Guarulhos). The bottom 5 states combined contribute 
under R$65K, less than 1.5% of SP's revenue alone.
Business implication: logistics, marketing, and seller acquisition 
efforts are likely most efficient when focused on the Southeast.

Q4: PAYMENT METHOD DISTRIBUTION
Credit card dominates: 76,795 payments (78% of total), R$12.54M in 
value, with an average of 3.5 installments per purchase. Boleto 
(a Brazilian bank-slip payment) is second at R$2.87M, but always 
paid in full (1 installment). Only credit card offers installment 
flexibility, suggesting it's a key enabler of larger purchases. 
3 "not_defined" payments with R$0 value are a minor data quality 
note, negligible impact.


Q5: REPEAT CUSTOMER RATE
Only 3% of customers (2,801 of 93,358) placed more than one order; 
97% are one-time buyers. This points to a significant retention 
problem — the business relies almost entirely on new customer 
acquisition rather than repeat purchases. A loyalty program or 
post-purchase engagement campaign could be a high-impact 
recommendation.
Business implication: this is likely the single biggest growth 
opportunity for the company — improving repeat purchase rate even 
slightly would have an outsized impact given how low it currently is.

Q6: SELLER PERFORMANCE (TOP 15 BY REVENUE)
Top 15 sellers by revenue are almost all based in SP or RJ, 
consistent with the geographic concentration seen in Q3. Late 
delivery rates among top sellers range from 4.6% to 14.4%, and 
sellers with higher late-delivery rates tend to have slightly 
lower average review scores (e.g., 14.4% late / 3.9 avg review 
vs. 4.6% late / 4.15 avg review). This suggests delivery 
reliability is a meaningful driver of customer satisfaction, 
worth confirming further in Q7 (delivery time analysis).


Q7: DELIVERY TIME VS ESTIMATE, BY STATE
Delivery speed varies hugely by region: SP averages just 8.8 days 
(fastest, highest volume), while remote northern states like RR 
(29.4 days), AP (27.2 days), and AM (26.4 days) take 3x longer. 
Notably, EVERY state delivers earlier than its estimated date — 
Olist's delivery estimates are consistently padded (SP: -10.4 
days early; RR: -16.6 days early). This suggests the company 
prioritizes avoiding "late" labels over giving customers accurate, 
tighter expectations. Business implication: delivery estimates 
could likely be tightened, especially in SP, without risking 
missed deadlines, which may improve perceived service speed.

Q8: CATEGORIES WITH WORST REVIEW SCORES
Lowest-rated categories (min. 30 orders): office_furniture (3.59), 
fashion_male_clothing (3.76), fixed_telephony (3.84). Notably, 
bed_bath_table appears here too (3.95 avg, 8,788 orders) despite 
being a top-3 revenue category (Q2) — a high-volume category with 
room to improve satisfaction, possibly linked to delivery/damage 
issues. No category in the bottom 10 reaches a 4.0 average, 
suggesting broad room for quality or expectation-setting 
improvements across the catalog.

Q9: MONTH-OVER-MONTH REVENUE GROWTH
Early months (Sep 2016-Jan 2017) show extreme % swings (up to 
1,025,573%) because the store had near-zero revenue at launch — 
not a meaningful trend, just a low base effect. From Feb 2017 
onward, growth stabilizes into realistic swings (-26% to +109%). 
Nov 2017 shows a clear spike (+52.4%), confirming the Black Friday 
pattern from Q1, followed by a sharp Dec 2017 drop (-26.5%), a 
common post-holiday pullback. By 2018, month-to-month growth is 
mostly single digits, suggesting the business reached a more 
mature, stable revenue pattern.
Note: Nov 2016 has zero orders (missing from results entirely) — 
minor data gap worth flagging in the README.

Q10: TOP 3 PRODUCTS PER CATEGORY (health_beauty, watches_gifts, bed_bath_table)
Used DENSE_RANK() to find each category's top 3 products by 
revenue. Notable: health_beauty's #1 product earns R$63,560 from 
just 186 orders, more revenue than bed_bath_table's #1 product 
(R$42,050 from 456 orders) despite far fewer sales — indicating a 
high-price hero product. Identifying and protecting stock/marketing 
for these top SKUs (9 products total across top 3 categories) could 
meaningfully impact category-level revenue.

Q11: CUSTOMER SEGMENTATION (RFM)
Used NTILE(4) to score customers on Recency, Frequency, and 
Monetary value, then grouped into Best (4-4-4), Worst (1-1-1), 
and Other. Best customers (1,745 people) spend 12.6x more than 
Worst customers (R$364.59 vs R$28.92 avg), but even Best customers 
average just 1.35 orders — barely more than one purchase. This 
refines the Q5 finding: the business has some high-value customers, 
but almost none are truly loyal/repeat buyers. Recommendation: 
target the "Best" segment (1,745 customers, big single-purchase 
spenders) with a second-purchase incentive, since they've already 
shown willingness to spend significantly.

Q12: CUMULATIVE REVENUE AND 3-MONTH MOVING AVERAGE
Total cumulative revenue reached R$13.22M by Aug 2018. The 3-month 
moving average smooths out seasonal noise (like the Nov 2017 Black 
Friday spike) and reveals a clearer underlying pattern: steady 
growth through 2017 (from ~R$50K/mo to ~R$486K/mo), continued 
climbing into a peak around May 2018 (~R$968K/mo), then a slight 
plateau/decline into mid-2018 (~R$854K/mo by Aug). This suggests 
revenue growth cooled after peaking in spring 2018, worth 
investigating further (e.g., seller growth, marketing spend, or 
market saturation) in a real business context.

Q13: COHORT RETENTION BY FIRST-PURCHASE MONTH
Tracked 3 sample cohorts (Jan 2017, Jun 2017, Jan 2018) from their 
first purchase month onward. All 3 show the same stark pattern: 
99%+ of customers never return after month 0. E.g., the Jan 2018 
cohort had 6,842 first-time buyers, but only 23 (0.3%) purchased 
again the following month, and numbers stay in the low double 
digits for all subsequent months. This confirms and visually 
proves the retention problem first identified in Q5 (3% repeat 
rate) and Q11 (even "best" customers average 1.35 orders) — the 
drop-off isn't gradual, it's immediate and near-total, consistent 
across different time periods (not a one-time anomaly).
Business implication: post-purchase engagement (email follow-up, 
loyalty incentives, second-purchase discount) should target 
customers within the first 30-60 days, since that's clearly the 
critical window before near-total drop-off.
